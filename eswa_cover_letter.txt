Dear Editor-in-Chief,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript entitled "When Feature Engineering Outperforms Deep Learning: Sparsity-Aware Direct Multi-Step Forecasting for Small-Scale Retail Demand" for consideration for publication in Expert Systems with Applications.

Why ESWA?

ESWA's emphasis on applied AI with substantive real-world validation makes it the right venue for this work. The manuscript evaluates a forecasting pipeline on a New York City coffee shop chain, combining LightGBM, Tweedie loss, and sparsity-aware feature engineering. The experiments support three operational outputs: a direct multi-step point forecast, conformal-calibrated P10-P90 intervals (80.6% coverage), and per-prediction TreeSHAP explanations. Those outputs can inform an inventory check. The manuscript does not report a production deployment or a what-if study.

What makes this contribution novel?

1. On a small daily panel (239 store-product series, 181 days), a gradient-boosted tree model with 53 engineered features has lower MAE than DeepAR, PatchTST, and TFT, by 23.0%, 15.2%, and 22.9%. The comparison is for this short-history regime. The manuscript distinguishes it from large-scale retail gradient boosting (Long et al., 2025) and from aftermarket intermittent feature engineering (Nathan et al., 2026).

2. I introduce a lightweight alternative to segment-specific modeling. Injecting four per-SKU sparsity statistics into one global model improves both sparse (-4.8% MAE) and dense (-1.1% MAE) predictions relative to one direct model. On overall MAE, R^2, and direction hit, that model beats a sparse/dense split. The split remains better on sparse MAE and on WAPE (0.432 versus 0.452). MAE is the primary metric.

3. The ablation study decomposes the performance gain and shows a counter-intuitive result: split models improve sparse accuracy but degrade dense accuracy because cross-segment information sharing is lost, whereas feature injection keeps the benefits of a unified model.

Fit with ESWA scope

The paper falls within ESWA's focus areas: (a) expert and intelligent systems applied in industry (retail operations), (b) practical guidelines for system development and management (the feature-engineering versus deep-learning findings give actionable guidance for practitioners), and (c) data mining and knowledge discovery (TreeSHAP together with sparsity labeling as a feature strategy).
